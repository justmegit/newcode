# Claims you can defend
*Challenge: "Can our advertising say this?"*
*Layer 2, Transform · dimensions: commercial engine, AI readiness · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger.csv`*

---

**Context.** A company selling an outdoor product to buyers in coastal areas.

**The challenge.** Ads and landing pages, **including pages we had built**, carried a precise performance figure and the word "certified". Both had travelled from sales collateral into live copy. Nobody had asked whether the company could defend them.

**What was underneath.** Copy now takes seconds to write. Checking it still means reading the source. We read the manufacturer's independent technical assessment in full.

- It did not contain the performance figure the ads used, anywhere.
- It attested **the design, not each unit** made. "Certified" said more than the document did.
- It held evidence nobody was using: a durability test that answers the question a buyer near the sea actually asks.

**The decision.** Say only what the document supports, and lead with the evidence that answers the buyer's real concern.

**Process, and who runs it.** Every technical claim is checked against the source document before it enters copy, and the check names the page and the line. We removed the rating and corrected the wording on the landing pages we managed, and kept both out of the ads. Then we built a series on the durability evidence, on real project photography, and ran it to a **prospecting audience**.

**Result.** Every unsupported claim we found was removed, from the ads and from the landing pages we managed. The buyer's real question, durability near the sea, is now answered with the evidence the company already had.

**What did not work.** Some of the wrong claims were on our own pages: we had copied them from the client's material without reading the source. And we cannot say whether the honest campaign sold better: measurement after the click was broken, so its commercial result could not be read.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. Which technical claims in your ads have been checked against the source document, and by whom?
2. Who signs off a claim before it ships, now that copy takes seconds to write?
3. Which concern do your buyers raise most, and which piece of your evidence answers it?
