# Recruitment as a routine, not an event
*Challenge: "Every vacancy starts from zero, and the hard ones go to an agency."*
*Layer 2, Transform · dimensions: processes, people and adoption, AI readiness · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger-v4.csv`*

---

**Context.** A services company with several locations and a steady need for mid-level technical staff. It hires the same kinds of profile year after year.

**The challenge.** Recruitment was planned at **about twelve vacancies a year**, and it came in peaks. Each vacancy was an event: an ad written from scratch, posted, closed, forgotten. Between peaks there was nothing, and when a profile was hard to find, it went to an agency.

**What was underneath.**

- The company was hiring the same profiles again and again, in different regions, and treating each time as new.
- Applications arrived through four channels, with CVs kept outside any retention rule.
- Screening had drifted into ranking candidates and inferring language skills from where they had studied. Under data-protection law, a ranking that people simply follow can count as an automated decision; under the AI Act, AI used to screen CVs is high-risk.

**The decision.** Make recurring roles permanent, make every opening visible, and keep AI out of selection.

- **Recurring regional roles.** A standing profile stays open for 30 days, closes for a week, and reopens, rotating across regions. Specific roles and spontaneous applications run alongside. Once the full set of recurring roles is approved, the model has capacity for **more than ten times** the processes the company had planned for.
- **Promotion built in.** Every opening produces **three drafts** of posts for review, each from a different angle, so every vacancy reaches the company's channels with content, not just a listing.
- **One platform.** A job site owned by the company, designed for consent, retention and anonymisation from the start, structured so that search engines read each role as a job.
- **No AI in selection.** Certifications are extracted from each CV by fixed rules and quoted word for word. No score, no ranking, no recommendation; candidates appear in order of arrival, with the original CV always at hand. AI writes promotion drafts, which a person reviews. It never touches a candidate.

**Process, and who runs it.** Each application goes to the area director for its region, who arranges interviews with HR. Our operator maintains the roles and reviews the promotion drafts. The company owns the platform and the data.

**Result.** Recurring roles are live and promoted with content every time they open. Senior roles are now recruited through the platform too; agencies remain for very specific cases. In its first months, the back office has received **more than three times** the applications the old system recorded in its entire history. **What was harder than planned.**
- HR has not adopted the platform yet. It works outside it: applications reach the right director, but nobody moves them through their stages in the system. Bringing HR on board is the next phase, and the hardest one. A tool does not create a routine.
- The full set of recurring roles is still going through approval, so most of the capacity is still capacity, not activity.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. Which roles do you hire for every year, and does each one still start from zero?
2. What did you pay agencies last year for profiles you will need again?
3. If a regulator asked how you shortlisted your last hire, could you show it?
