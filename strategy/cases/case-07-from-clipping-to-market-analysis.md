# From press clipping to market analysis
*Challenge: "We get a monthly press clipping. Nobody does anything with it."*
*Layer 2, Transform · dimensions: business context, data and systems · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger-v4.csv` and `evidence-ledger-isq.csv`*

---

**Context.** A mid-size technical-services firm in Spain, in a market where much of the work is awarded through public tenders. Management received a monthly clipping of what competitors, clients and partners had posted.

**The challenge.** The clipping arrived every month, and it never led to a single action.

**What was underneath.** It was a list. Posts grouped by company, each with a title and a link, and no view on what any of it meant. It was also wrong in ways nobody checked: in one two-month edition, **41%** of the posts predated the period it covered, and a competitor was filed as a client. And it only read what competitors chose to say about themselves. In this market, the work that matters is won in public tenders and in accreditations, and competitors rarely post about either.

**The decision.** Stop describing what competitors say and start reading what they do. The monthly report now crosses three things: what companies post, what the public procurement register shows they won, and what the accreditation body shows they gained. Public procurement became the beacon: a daily feed of every tender and award, filtered down to what falls inside the company's own range of services. Only public company pages and public registers are read. Each section ends with what it means for the company, and the report answers the same standing questions from management every month, even when nothing has moved. It informs management; the decisions stay with management.

**Process, and who runs it.** Code collects the sources and computes every figure. AI reads the posts and drafts the analysis, and a model from a different vendor reviews the draft against a written rubric. A person checks it against the sources and approves it before it goes out. The themes it surfaces feed the company's own content plan.

**Result.** On the first pass, **4 of 4** public awards to competitors had not been mentioned by the competitors anywhere. The readings changed in kind: a large international competitor that never posts about Spain turned out to be winning public contracts there; a mid-size competitor with little visible activity was accumulating awards without announcing any of them. The report also refused a **+167%** jump in competitor activity that came from how earlier months had been collected, not from the market.

**What was harder than planned.** The sources do not stand still. Platforms limit collection, public registers change format, an AI service times out on the day the report is due. Every month needs a fallback route and a person who checks each figure against its source before anything goes out; that check is what makes the report safe to act on. The harder step is the last one: we cannot yet point to a decision that came from the report. Turning a reading into a decision inside the company is the work that remains.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. When did your market monitoring last lead to an action, and which one?
2. Does it read what competitors say, or what they win?
3. When the numbers change, can you tell a change in the market from a change in how the data was collected?
