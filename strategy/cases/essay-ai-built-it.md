# Built in weeks. Judgement didn't come with it.
*Essay · anonymized single-client example · draft v3, 23 Oct 2026 · revised after two red-team passes*
*⚠ Do not publish while the relationship that inspired it is active. It describes one company, anonymized; re-test for re-identification before publishing.*

---

A premium consumer-services company brought its digital work in-house. A new website, an email programme and its own social channels all went live **within weeks**. By all appearances the code was generated with AI-assisted tools. The result looked expensive: serif headlines, generous white space, interactive tools, an editorial section.

It is a fair decision. Execution has become cheap, and anyone who sells it by the hour should notice.

What did not come with the tools is the part that decides whether any of it pays.

**Measurement.** An inspection of the new site found **no ad-platform tags**: no Google tag, tag manager or Meta pixel. Unless they are added elsewhere, paid traffic sent there loses the signal the bidding runs on, and nobody sees the cost until the numbers drift.

**Language.** Passages of the copy **did not read as native**. A native buyer notices in the first line.

**Claims and the handoff.** A promise is a claim, and it should be checked like one. In one lead we traced, an acknowledgement sent shortly after the request promised contact within a couple of business days. The first reply came **more than a week** after the request. Of the contacts created in that period, **all but one** had no follow-up recorded in the system that captured them. The follow-up may have happened somewhere else. Nobody could show it.

None of this is a tooling problem. In each gap, no owner was visible: for the signal the campaigns optimise on, for the copy before it ships, for a promise checked against the operation, for a lead and the date it is answered.

That is the layer AI has not commoditised. Digital projects often stall there, at the two steps everyone skips: the process that has to change, and the people who have to work differently on Monday.

Technology does not transform a company. Adopted technology does.

*From problem to process.*
