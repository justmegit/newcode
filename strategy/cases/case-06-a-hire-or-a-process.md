# They asked for a hire. The work had no process to hand over.
*Challenge: "I need to replace our communications person."*
*Layer 2, Transform, with Layer 3, Advise, alongside · dimensions: processes, people and adoption · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger-isq.csv`*

---

**Context.** The Iberian operation of an international services group. Its customers buy because a regulation, a deadline or an incident requires it. Communications ran on one person: social media, job ads, internal notices. The role was changing hands, and the country manager asked us to find a replacement.

**The challenge.** Fill the vacancy before the work stopped.

**What was underneath.** The work only existed while someone did it by hand, and nobody could see when it stopped. An automated staff notice had sent nothing since April while reporting success every day. No log, no alert, no owner. Hiring someone new would have handed them the same invisible failures.

**The decision.** No full-time hire. A junior operator from our side runs the daily work; a senior consultant redesigns each task as a process the client owns. Two rules hold it together:

1. Code computes, AI drafts, a person approves.
2. Every process reports its own failures to a person, in plain language, before anyone has to ask.

AI use was limited to tools the company had already approved, and client documents stayed inside the company's own systems. The design started from those limits.

**Process, and who runs it.** Each task became a process with an owner, a cadence and a check. A quiet week raises a flag. The operator runs it, the country manager approves, we maintain it. Each role has written operating instructions.

**Result.** The communications function has run since February without a hire, at about **40%** of the full cost of a junior hire, employer charges included. Every scheduled notice is now logged, and a missed one is visible within a week rather than months.

**The way out.** The company is not locked in. Manuals and roles are written down for each process. Bringing the work in-house would take one junior hire for the daily processing, and training the company's IT director, who already knows the office platform, in workflow automation and AI models so the technical side has an owner.

**What was harder than planned.** Automation fails quietly too, including ours. A step in one of the new processes stopped while still showing as active. It now reports to a person when it goes quiet: the same rule we set for the client, applied to our own work.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. Which recurring task would you notice stopping, and how long would it take you?
2. Before you hire for a role, can you write down the process the person would run?
3. Which of your automations would tell you if it had stopped working?
