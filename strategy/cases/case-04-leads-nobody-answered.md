# The leads were there. Nobody owned the next step.
*Challenge: "We need more leads."*
*Layer 1, Assess · dimensions: commercial engine, people and adoption · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger.csv` · a case about what we did not fix*

---

**Context.** A company selling high-ticket installations to households. Marketing was ours; sales was the client's own team.

**The challenge.** The request was the usual one: more leads. The media was asked to fix a sales problem.

**What was underneath.** We followed every paid contact of the half-year into the CRM.

- **81.6%** of paid-social contacts had no next step recorded in the CRM: no call, no meeting, no quote, no owner. Some follow-up may have happened outside it; nobody could show it.
- In paid search it was **42.6%**, at a cost per contact **7.6x** higher. These were the most expensive contacts the company bought, and almost half of them stopped there.
- **41%** of that paid-search group had asked for product information. These were people asking to be sold to.

More leads would have meant more of the same: paid contacts arriving into a process that did not exist.

**The decision.** We took the report and its recommendations to management, and were careful not to turn it into an attack on the sales team: before any budget increase, every paid contact needs an owner and a date for the next step, visible in the CRM. The funnel reports and dashboards stayed open in the CRM, step by step, for anyone to read.

**What happened.** Nothing changed in sales. The data was there, the recommendations were on the table, and the redesign was never made.

**What we learned.** In this argument, the marketing partner is the weakest voice. Lead quality is whatever the sales team says it is, and the sales team can argue what it likes. Only a parallel contact with the prospects themselves can settle it, and nobody had agreed to one. A funnel report shows where contacts stop. It cannot show why. So the check has to be agreed at the start, before anyone has a position to defend: who will speak to a sample of prospects, and whose reading counts.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. For every paid contact last quarter, who owned the next step, and how many days did it take?
2. Which of your contacts asked to be sold to, and what happened to them?
3. When marketing and sales disagree about lead quality, who picks up the phone and asks the prospects?
