# Decide what a lead is worth before you decide the budget
*Challenge: "Do we cut the media or put more in?"*
*Layer 1, Assess · Layer 3, Advise · dimensions: commercial engine, business context · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger.csv`*

---

**Context.** A company selling high-ticket home projects, with a long sales cycle and most new contacts coming from paid media.

**The challenge.** Management kept asking one question in two forms: are we spending too much, or not enough? Each month the answer depended on how the last few weeks had felt.

**What was underneath.** There was no rule to decide with. The company knew what a lead cost. It did not know what a lead was worth, so every budget conversation was about cost alone.

**The decision.** Work out the value of a lead from the company's own funnel, then set a ceiling management could hold us to.

- **5.1%** of leads in the home market became a sale. Marketing cost **4.6%** of the average deal value.
- At gross margin, a lead in the best segment would produce **more than seven times** what it cost. That is an estimate from the company's own conversion rate and project values, and we said so.
- We proposed a cost-per-lead ceiling of **about one fifth** of that margin. Under it, buy more. Over it, stop and find out why.

**Process, and who runs it.** One number, reviewed monthly with management against the CRM, not against the ad platforms. The question in the meeting changed from "how many leads?" to "are we under the ceiling, and are the leads turning into quotes?"

**Result.** In the month after the ceiling was proposed, **7.9%** of new contacts had reached a quote or a later stage by the time of writing. When the client later cut media to **about a third**, daily contact volume fell only to **80%** of the previous month's pace over the first three weeks.

**What did not work.** The cut came before the ceiling had a full quarter of data behind it, so the rule was never tested on the decision it was built for. Part of the contact volume after the cut is the tail of earlier spend. And the fact that volume held so well suggests some of the earlier spend was buying contacts the company would have had anyway: exactly what a ceiling is meant to reveal sooner.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. What is a lead worth to you, at margin, and who calculated it?
2. What cost per lead would make you stop buying, and is it written down?
3. In your last budget meeting, was the discussion about cost alone?
