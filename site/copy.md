## 2. THE PROBLEM (Approach)

### Why most digital and AI initiatives fail

It's rarely the technology. Tools get bought and never used. Pilots impress and never scale. Agencies deliver campaigns; consultants deliver slides; software vendors deliver licenses — and eighteen months later, the way the company actually works hasn't changed.

Transformation fails at the two layers everyone skips: **processes** that were never redesigned, and **people** who were never brought along.

### From problem to process

Bluewright works the full chain — **problem → strategy → execution → process** — and only considers the job done when the new way of working runs with your team trained and owning it. Not a report. Not a tool. A process that works.

## 5. WORK (case studies)

### Transformation, not deliverables

Eleven challenges, drawn from four engagements. Clients are not named: administrations change, and the lessons do not.

#### Knowing what you are buying

**Scenarios, not a budget.** *"How much should we invest in Spain, and what would each level buy?"* A northern European manufacturer's board received two complete scenarios instead of a budget request: channels, what each would and would not produce, and the fee, around a programme that gives prescribers a reason to name the brand. *Harder than planned:* A clear choice does not set a date. The board has not decided yet.

**Check what the algorithm learns from.** *"The platforms say the account is healthy. I don't know what the media is buying."* 99.99% of the value the search platform reported was a default or a duplicate. We made the signal true before touching the budget, and every launch now starts with a test lead. *Harder than planned:* A tracking failure ran for 20 days before we found it. The check that would have caught it did not exist yet.

**Decide what a lead is worth.** *"Do we cut the media or put more in?"* The company knew what a lead cost, not what it was worth. We worked it out from its own funnel and proposed a ceiling management could hold us to. *Harder than planned:* Media was cut before the ceiling had a quarter of data behind it, so the rule was never tested on that decision.

#### Work that should not depend on one person

**A hire or a process.** *"I need to replace our communications person."* An automated notice had sent nothing for months while reporting success. Instead of filling the role, we redesigned each task as a process the client owns, with a person approving and failures reported before anyone asks. It has run since February at about 40% of the cost of a junior hire. *Harder than planned:* Automation fails quietly too, including ours. That process now reports to a person when it goes quiet.

**A catalogue nobody could update.** *"Online sales have fallen. Why invest in a channel that is shrinking?"* Online revenue had fallen 60% in a year, and each new course date needed a developer. We treated the shop as a symptom: the business team now publishes its own dates. *Harder than planned:* Go-live slipped two weeks waiting on a partner's feedback. The plan needed a written deadline for outside input.

**Recruitment as a routine.** *"Every vacancy starts from zero, and the hard ones go to an agency."* The company kept hiring the same profiles from scratch. Standing regional roles now reopen on a cycle, promoted with content each time, with no AI in selection. Applications are more than three times what the old system recorded in its entire history. *Harder than planned:* HR has not adopted the platform yet. That is the next phase, and the hardest one.

#### Reading what you already have

**From press clipping to market analysis.** *"We get a monthly press clipping. Nobody does anything with it."* The report now crosses what companies post with what public registers show they won. On the first pass, 4 of 4 public awards to competitors had not been mentioned by the competitors anywhere. *Harder than planned:* The sources do not stand still, and a reading still has to become a decision inside the company.

**The next sale is already in your data.** *"Who should we be calling, and aren't?"* Cross-selling ran on memory. We rebuilt the enrolment history into a standing list of who to call. In its first week, all 30 renewal opportunities on it had already lapsed. *Harder than planned:* Making history usable took two passes, and our automation once acted on a decision that belonged to the client. It was reverted the next day.

#### What the company says, and who follows through

**Claims you can defend.** *"Can our advertising say this?"* Ads carried a figure and a word the source document did not support, some of them on pages we had built. We removed them and led with the evidence the buyer actually needed. *Harder than planned:* We cannot say whether the honest campaign sold better: measurement after the click was broken.

**Content that stalled in approval.** *"We pay for two posts a week and publish one."* Piece-by-piece approval held output at about one a week. Decisions in layers, the service catalogue as a filter and AI drafting under human review brought it to two a week, each with its approval on record. *Harder than planned:* Contributions from the field are a change of culture, still under way. Generated illustrations are the buffer until then.

**The leads were there. Nobody owned the next step.** *"We need more leads."* 81.6% of paid-social contacts had no next step recorded in the CRM. The report reached management and the data stayed open to all, and nothing changed. *Harder than planned:* Lead quality is whatever sales says it is, unless someone agrees at the start to ask the prospects themselves.
