# BLUEWRIGHT — Brand Book
**v1 · July 2026 · source of truth for every visual and verbal decision**

---

## 1. The name

**Bluewright.** *Wright* is the maker who builds — shipwright, playwright, wheelwright. Not a consultant who advises from outside, but a craftsman who builds the thing and stays until it runs. *Blue* carries deliberate continuity from Blue Serenity: long-standing clients recognize the lineage, new markets see only a strong name.

Always one word, no space, no hyphen. Written **BLUEWRIGHT** in logo and nav (letterspaced), **Bluewright** in body text. Never "BlueWright", never "Blue Wright".

**Descriptor (always adjacent to the mark in formal use):** Digital Strategy & Transformation · Iberia
**Tagline:** *From problem to process.* (PT: *De problema a processo.* / ES: *De problema a proceso.*)
**Signature line for the founder:** A practice by Alexandre Pessoa.

## 2. The mark — the wright angle

The identity element is a **right angle**: the carpenter's square, the shipwright's set square, the engineer's tool for checking that something is true. It is the visual pun at the heart of the name — *wright / right angle* — and the promise of the practice: things built square.

Construction: two strokes meeting at the lower-left corner, forming ⌐ rotated — left stroke vertical, bottom stroke horizontal, meeting at bottom-left. Stroke weight 20–25% of the square's side. Always brass on dark, or navy on light. Never filled, never rounded, never rotated at an angle.

Uses: as the icon before the logotype; as the bullet in eyebrows and section labels; oversized as a graphic device in monograms and covers; as the favicon.

**Never:** add a third stroke (it becomes a frame), place it inside a circle, apply gradients, or animate it spinning.

## 3. Colour

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Navy** | `#122C4A` | Primary. Backgrounds of authority, headers, footers, the mark on light. |
| **Harbor** | `#0C1F35` | Deepest tone. Gradient ends, footer, hero depth. |
| **Brass** | `#C9A15A` | The single accent. The angle, key CTAs, emphasis, the price. Used sparingly — brass is the jewel, not the fabric. |
| **Steel** | `#3E7CB1` | Secondary accent. Links, small labels, diagrams. |
| **Fog** | `#EFF3F7` | Alternating light section background. |
| **Ink** | `#20293A` | Body text on light. |
| **Grey** | `#5A6472` | Secondary text, captions. |
| **Line** | `#D8E0E8` | Borders, rules, dividers. |

Ratio discipline: roughly **70% navy/white structure · 25% fog and greys · 5% brass**. If brass stops feeling special, there is too much of it.

Accessibility: brass on navy passes AA for large text and UI, not for body copy — never set paragraphs in brass on navy. On light backgrounds use `#8A6A2F` (dark brass) for small text instead of `#C9A15A`.

## 4. Typography

Three faces, three jobs. All available free on Google Fonts, so no licensing friction.

**Newsreader** (display, weight 300–500) — an editorial serif with real personality: headlines, section titles, product names, pull quotes. Set at tight leading (1.1–1.15), often in italic for emphasis. It carries the "senior counsel who writes" register.

**Archivo** (UI/body, weight 400–600, width 100; width 125 for the logotype) — a grotesque with a confident industrial build. Body copy, navigation, buttons, cards. The expanded width at 125 gives the logotype its engineered stance.

**IBM Plex Mono** (utility, 400–500) — eyebrows, labels, process-rail nodes, data captions. Uppercase, letterspaced `.13em`. This is the engineering voice: the instrument reading, the specification label.

Scale (desktop): hero `clamp(38px,6vw,64px)` · h2 `clamp(28px,3.6vw,40px)` · h3 `23–26px` · body `17px/1.65` · small `14.5–15.5px` · eyebrow `12.5px`.

Rules: sentence case everywhere except mono labels (uppercase) and the logotype. Never letterspace Newsreader. Never set body copy in mono. Measure caps at 68 characters.

## 5. Structural devices

**The process rail** — Problem → Strategy → Execution → Process, as connected nodes with the final node inverted (navy fill, brass text). This is the methodology made visible and is the page's signature element. It appears on the site, in decks, and in proposals. The sequence is never reordered and never abbreviated to three steps.

**The eyebrow** — mono label preceded by the wright angle, in brass. Every major section opens with one.

**Alternating bands** — white → fog → white → navy. Navy bands are reserved for the two moments that must feel like a change of register: the Assessment and the Contact. Overusing navy flattens that effect.

## 6. Voice

**Register:** senior, plain, unhurried. The tone of someone who has seen the failure mode before and is not selling. Short declaratives. Concrete nouns. No adjectives doing the work of evidence.

**Signature moves that define the voice:**
- Name the failure before the offer ("Tools get bought and never used.")
- State prices and terms plainly, without hedging.
- Say what we don't do ("including what *not* to do", "no follow-up sequence if the answer is no").
- Turn the solo model into an advantage without ever saying "freelancer" ("No pyramid of juniors. No hours to sell.").

**Banned vocabulary:** synergy, leverage (as a verb), cutting-edge, holistic, end-to-end solutions, "we're passionate about", journey (in the marketing sense), unlock, empower, game-changing, "in today's fast-paced world".

**Languages:** English is the primary language of the brand — website, offers, proposals, one-pagers. Spanish and Portuguese versions are derived when a client relationship justifies it. Never machine-translate the tagline; the three approved versions are fixed.

## 7. Applications — quick rules

**Website:** navy hero with the angle beside the headline; brass CTA primary, ghost CTA secondary; process rail in the Approach section.
**One-pagers / PDFs:** navy header band with a 3px brass rule underneath; two columns; price in Newsreader at 15pt+ in brass; navy footer.
**CV:** same header band, no brass rule inside the body, mono only for dates.
**LinkedIn:** banner = navy with oversized brass angle, logotype, tagline. Profile photo stays photographic — never overlay the mark on a face.
**Email signature:** logotype in Archivo 125 width, descriptor in grey, no image logo (keeps deliverability clean).

## 8. What would break the brand

Stock photography of handshakes, glass skyscrapers, or people pointing at whiteboards. Gradients other than the navy→harbor hero. Rounded corners above 3px. More than one accent colour. Icon sets with rounded, friendly strokes. Anything that would look at home on a generic SaaS landing page — the whole point of this identity is that it looks like it belongs to a person with three decades of judgement, not to a template.
