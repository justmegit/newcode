# A market report that knows when it cannot tell
*Challenge: "The report says competitor activity has jumped. Is that true?"*
*Layer 2, Transform · dimensions: data and systems, business context · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger-isq.csv`*

---

**Context.** The Spanish subsidiary of a European technical-services group, whose customers buy because a regulation, a deadline or an incident requires it, often through public tenders. Management received a monthly report on what competitors were doing.

**The challenge.** The report was compiled by hand from what competitors posted. Management could not tell whether a change in the numbers was a change in the market.

**What was underneath.** The report measured the wrong things and counted some of them twice.

- It counted posts by competitors' clients and executives as competitor activity. That inflated one month's figures by **7%**.
- It only read what competitors chose to say about themselves. What they won in public tenders, or the accreditations they gained or lost, never reached it.
- When the collection method changed, the numbers changed with it, and nothing in the report said so.

**The decision.** Stop measuring what competitors say and start measuring what they do. The report now crosses four sources: competitors' own pages, the public procurement register, changes in accreditations, and its own history. A person validates it before management sees it. When the data cannot support a conclusion, the report says so.

**Process, and who runs it.** Collection runs weekly and the report monthly. Our operator validates each report against its sources before it goes to the country manager. Any change in how data is collected is noted in the report itself.

**Result.** In its first months, the report surfaced public contract awards that competitors had not mentioned anywhere. It also refused to report a **+167%** jump in competitor activity, because the jump came from how earlier months had been collected, not from the market. Management got one fewer false alarm and one new source of real information.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. When your market monitoring shows a change, can you tell a change in the market from a change in how the data was collected?
2. Does your competitor tracking read what competitors say, or what they win?
3. Who validates your market report before management acts on it?
