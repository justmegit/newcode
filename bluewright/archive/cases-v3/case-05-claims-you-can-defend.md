# Case 5 — Claims you can defend
*Validates: the thesis (AI made execution cheap, not judgement) · Layer 2 · Dimensions: commercial engine, AI readiness*
*Status: draft v3, 23 Oct 2026 · anonymized · revised after two red-team passes and re-identification review · every figure traced in `evidence-ledger.csv`*

---

**Context.** The same premium outdoor-living installer, selling a product into coastal markets.

**Problem.** Ads and landing pages, **including pages we had built**, carried a precise wind-resistance rating and the word "certified". Both had travelled from sales collateral into live copy. Copy now takes seconds to produce. Checking it still means reading the source.

**What we found.** We read the manufacturer's independent technical assessment, in full.

- It contained **no wind-speed figure**. Wind performance was attested by reference to a standard other than the one in the market where the ads ran.
- It attests **the design, not each unit** made. "Certified" overstated what the document says.
- And it held evidence our ads and pages were not using: a corrosion test, the question that matters for a metal structure near the sea.

**Strategy.** Say only what the document supports, and lead with the evidence that answers the coastal buyer's concern.

**Execution.** Removed the rating and corrected the attestation wording on the landing pages we managed, and kept both out of the ad copy before it shipped. Built a coastal series on the corrosion evidence, on real project photography with no generated imagery, ordered from benefit to attribute to close, and ran it to a **prospecting audience**.

**Result.** We removed every unsupported claim we found. The commercial result, on one basis throughout (form leads counted by the platform, against warm retargeting the month before), is honest and mixed: **41% lower** cost per lead after nine days, **17% higher** after 18, with no new lead after day eight. Link click-through was **1.01%**, but only **9%** of clicks registered a landing-page view while most leads came through the platform's own form. Measurement after the click is not yet clean, and that is the next thing to fix before reading anything more into the numbers.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. Which technical claims in your ads have been checked against the source document, and by whom?
2. Who signs off a claim before it ships, now that copy takes seconds to write?
3. Which concern do your buyers raise most, and which piece of your evidence answers it?

### LinkedIn version (~120 words)

The ads said the product was certified to a precise wind speed. Some of those pages were ours.

We read the source document in full. There was no wind-speed figure in it, and it attested the design, not each unit made. The claim had travelled from a sales deck into live copy, and nobody, us included, had checked it against the source.

The same document held a corrosion test: the evidence that matters for metal on the coast. Our own pages were not using it.

We removed the claim and built the campaign on the evidence. The early cost per lead beat warm retargeting, then lost the lead. We cannot read the results yet: measurement after the click is not clean.

AI writes claims in seconds. Someone still has to read the source.
