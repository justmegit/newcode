# What the company may say, whoever drafts it
*Challenge: "Can we let AI propose what we publish?"*
*Layer 2, Transform · dimensions: AI readiness, processes · draft v4, 24 Oct 2026 · anonymized · figures in `evidence-ledger-isq.csv`*

---

**Context.** The Spanish subsidiary of a European technical-services group. What it sells is its signature: an inspection, a certificate, a statement that something complies. In a business like that, a claim it cannot stand behind costs more than a missed post.

**The challenge.** Management wanted the speed of AI-assisted content planning without putting the company's name to things it does not do.

**What was underneath.** We checked an AI-assisted content plan against the company's published service catalogue. **5 of 9** proposed topics claimed services the company does not sell. The drafts were fluent because AI writes well about a market, and the market is wider than what the company is accredited to do.

**The decision.** The catalogue decides what the company can say in the first person, and it decides before a topic enters the plan, not after a draft is written. AI may propose and draft. It may not produce a figure, a regulatory reference or a service claim that a person has not checked against a source.

**Process, and who runs it.** Every proposed topic is checked against the service catalogue before it is planned. Drafts that need a figure or a regulation say so and go to a person. The country manager approves each piece and can edit or stop it until it goes out.

**Result.** None of the five topics was published. Since the check moved to the start of the plan, a topic that claims a service the company does not sell no longer reaches a draft. [FILL: confirm with the operator that no unsupported claim has been published since.]

**What did not work.** The check came after the first plan, not before it. More than half of that plan had to be thrown away. The rule exists because of that plan.

---

**Three questions to ask your team tomorrow**
1. Does your content claim anything your service catalogue cannot back?
2. When AI drafts for you, who checks the figures and the regulatory references, and against what?
3. Is the check at the start of your content process, or at the end?
